Chargeback Rates & Dispute Costs — Market Data

A merchant that crosses a card network threshold does not get a warning letter and a grace period worth planning around. It gets a monitoring program, per-dispute fees, and a clock. That is why chargeback economics are measured in ratios first and dollars second: the ratio decides whether the fees start at all.

The figures below pull the two halves together — the published network thresholds that trigger monitoring, and the published cost research on what each dispute actually costs once it lands. Read the ratio columns before the dollar columns; the dollars only matter after the ratio has already gone wrong.

Methodology

Included: published card-network monitoring thresholds and published cost research (Mastercard with Javelin and Datos Insights, 2025–2026). Excluded: vendor marketing tables of industry chargeback rates — the networks do not publish industry-level chargeback rates, and vendor tables use incompatible definitions, so none are reproduced here. Where a figure is a projection, it is labelled as one. Threshold figures describe the 2026 VAMP regime; the older VDMP figures are kept only as labelled historical context.

Trend labels: “Baseline (October 2026) — trend tracking starts with the next refresh” means no prior-period figure from the same source was available this session, so no movement is claimed. Where a prior figure is shown, the trend compares the current figure with that prior figure from the same source.
