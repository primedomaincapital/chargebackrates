Friendly Fraud: The Dispute Category Eating Merchant Margins

The cardholder is real. The transaction was authorized. The dispute is still filed. First-party misuse has become the dominant shape of chargeback volume — and the dispute system was not built to adjudicate it.

Prime Domain Capital  ·  October 2026

Classic fraud is a stranger with a stolen card number. Friendly fraud is the cardholder themselves — disputing a charge they authorized, whether from forgetfulness, buyer's remorse, a subscription they meant to cancel, or deliberate abuse of the dispute process. The transaction data looks identical to a legitimate sale right up until the dispute arrives.

This is what makes first-party misuse so corrosive. Every tool built to stop criminal fraud — AVS, CVV, 3-D Secure — passes a friendly-fraud transaction cleanly, because the buyer is who they claim to be. The fraud happens after authorization, in the gap between delivery and the cardholder's decision to reverse the charge instead of requesting a refund.

“The hardest fraud to fight is the one where the criminal is the customer.”

Why representment usually fails

To win a dispute, a merchant must produce compelling evidence: proof of delivery, IP and device data, prior undisputed transaction history, evidence the cardholder used the product or service. Visa's Compelling Evidence 3.0 framework formalized these requirements — and in doing so, revealed how few merchants systematically collect the data needed to meet them.

The economics compound the problem. Assembling evidence costs staff time whether the dispute is $20 or $2,000, which means low-ticket merchants rationally abandon representment — and serial abusers learn which merchants never fight back. Dispute behavior is adaptive: every uncontested chargeback teaches the ecosystem that disputing works.

The measurement gap

Here is the uncomfortable truth for the industry: nobody can say with precision what share of chargeback volume is first-party misuse versus true fraud, because the reason codes cardholders select rarely describe what actually happened. A buyer who forgot about a subscription files “fraud — card absent” because it is the fastest path to a reversal. The data downstream is polluted at the source.

Cleaning that data — benchmarking friendly-fraud rates by vertical, tracking how dispute reasons drift, measuring which evidence actually wins — is a research program the industry needs and no one has built.
